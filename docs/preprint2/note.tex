\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Smoothed-particle impact simulations are routinely run at the coarsest
resolution that fits the budget, and the resulting discretisation error
is folded into the likelihood as extra variance. We show, on a
96-point production campaign for the DART impact on Dimorphos, that
this is the wrong operation. Re-running nine of the design points at
half the particle spacing moves the momentum enhancement in the
\emph{same direction at all nine points} (median ratio $1.139$, sign
test $p = 0.002$): the discretisation error is a bias, not scatter.
Carrying it as variance leaves the posterior centred on the coarse
prediction, so the interior parameters absorb the shift. Correcting it
instead with a Kennedy--O'Hagan autoregressive surrogate moves the
inferred matrix strength from $18.7$~Pa to $159.7$~Pa, a $1.88\sigma$
shift, and the shift lands on the one direction the single observable
can constrain at all. Neither value is converged; the point is that the
choice of resolution, not the data, selects between them.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem}

A kinetic impact leaves one well-measured scalar, the momentum
enhancement $\beta$. Inferring interior properties from it requires a
forward model, and that model is discretised. The usual treatment adds
a resolution term $\sigma_{\text{res}}$ to the error budget.

That treatment assumes the discretisation error behaves like noise. If
it is instead a systematic offset, widening the likelihood does not
move the posterior's centre: the parameters must absorb the offset.
One then believes a wrong interior precisely because it reproduces the
right $\beta$.

\section{Measurement}

The production campaign is 96 Latin-hypercube points over boulder
porosity $\alpha_b$, matrix strength $Y_0$ and boulder volume fraction
$f$, run with a GPU SPH solver (FP64, Wendland C2, Tillotson,
P--$\alpha$ porosity, Grady--Kipp damage, self-gravity) on a triaxial
$88.5 \times 87.0 \times 58.0$~m body at $7.0$~m base spacing. Nine
points of a maximin subset, fixed before any data existed, were
re-run at $3.5$~m: $441$ GPU-hours.

Table~\ref{tab:bias} gives the result: $9$ of $9$ in the same
direction, median ratio $1.139$. A one-sided sign test gives
$p = 2^{-9} = 0.002$. The coarse campaign underestimates $\beta$ by
about $14\%$, systematically.

\section{Correction}

Because the error is systematic it can be modelled. We fit a
Kennedy--O'Hagan autoregressive surrogate,
$y_{\text{fine}}(x) = \rho\, f_{\text{coarse}}(x) + \delta(x)$,
on the nine nested points, obtaining $\rho = 0.820$ for $\beta$ and
$\rho = 1.180$ for log ejecta mass, both interior to the search grid.

Leave-one-out across the nine points, against the fine value: RMS
error falls from $0.656$ uncorrected to $0.262$ corrected, a factor
$2.51$, and the corrected surrogate wins at all nine points
individually.

\section{Consequence for the inference}

Both posteriors were then computed in a single run sharing the design,
the observable transform, the residual correlation, the error model,
the grid and the likelihood. Only the surrogate mean differs, so any
difference cannot be attributed elsewhere.

The inferred matrix strength moves by a factor of $8.6$
(Table~\ref{tab:mode}). The shift falls on $Y_0$, which is the only
direction this observable constrains: a Fisher decomposition over the
same 96 points gives posterior widths of $0.142$, $0.930$ and $1.000$
of the prior along the three principal directions, with the learnable
one aligned with $Y_0$ at $90$ of $96$ points. A shift along an
unconstrained axis would not matter. This one does.

\section{What this does not show}

The fine runs are not converged either: differences grow with
refinement rather than settling, so $159.7$~Pa is what one further
step of refinement says, not where the sequence lands. We therefore
report both numbers and treat neither as the interior of Dimorphos.
The modes for $\alpha_b$ and $f$ sit against their prior bounds and
are not estimates. The nested design was planned at twelve points and
nine were run, so $\delta(x)$ is extrapolated near the design edges.

The claim is narrower than a value and, we think, more useful: in this
class of problem the resolution decision is not a nuisance absorbed by
the error bars. It selects the answer, and it should be reported as
part of the result.
