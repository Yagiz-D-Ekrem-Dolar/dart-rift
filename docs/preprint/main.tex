\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}\enspace
Five completed, validity-checked SPH pilot simulations were run in a
fixed DART impact scene to measure model sensitivity before production
inference. At matrix cohesion $\Yzero=10$, 500, and 5000 Pa, simulated
momentum enhancement is $\beta=3.7480$, 2.6247, and 1.8738, respectively;
the corresponding recorded ejecta masses are
$1.964\times10^7$, $3.649\times10^6$, and $7.516\times10^5$ kg.
At 10 Pa, enabling the tested matrix-tension law reduces
$\beta$ to 1.0807, while a 25 m impact offset gives 3.6650 and
changes model ejecta mass by 18\%. A three-point power law for $\beta-1$
has slope $-0.1791$ and maximum relative residual 0.1198; its
scene-conditioned interpolation gives 20.1 Pa at the mass-adjusted
observed $\beta$. That interpolation is not an interior-strength
measurement or posterior. We register a 96-point coarse design and 12
nested fine reruns, and identify unresolved numerical and
observation-definition checks that prevent a defensible production
posterior or numerical Hera forecast at present.

\section{Introduction}
The DART experiment measured a change in Dimorphos's orbit, constraining
the momentum transferred to the body \cite{daly2023,cheng2023}. Translating
that response into interior structure requires a forward model whose
material laws, target geometry, resolution, and observation operators
are controlled. We use DART-RIFT to study three interior coordinates:
block distension $\aB$, matrix cohesion $\Yzero$, and block volume
fraction $f$. A match by one simulated scene is evidence about that
scene, not an identified three-parameter interior.

This paper reports the completed pilot measurements first. It then
specifies the production design and claim checks that were fixed
before reading the production pool. The pilot runs themselves predate
this paper and are not presented as blind predictions.

\section{Simulation and comparison}
\subsection{Fixed scene and controlled changes}
The reference target is a triaxial ellipsoid with semiaxes
$(88.5,87,58)$ m and calibrated mass
$4.2980\times10^9$ kg (Figure~\ref{fig:scene}).
The three-sphere impactor has mass 579.4 kg,
speed 6144.9 m\,s$^{-1}$, and nominal incidence $17^\circ$ from the
surface normal. The pilot cohesion comparison fixes
$(\aB,f)=(1.15,0.25)$, the random seed 20260906, 7 m particle spacing,
a 1.0 s transition time, and a 600 s endpoint
\repo{docs/truba/PROTOKOL-DO-DAYANIM-ONSELI.md}{[pilot protocol]}.
Figure~\ref{fig:cascade} places those two fixed times inside the
sequence a run actually integrates.
DO1 and DO2 change only $\Yzero$ from the DY2 reference. DC enables
one selected matrix-tension law at 10 Pa; DN moves the impact point
25 m at 10 Pa. The production model keeps that tension law disabled
\repo{docs/adr/ADR-0056-a105-matris-cekme-dayanimi.md}{[model decision]}.

All four new branches completed and passed the repository's validity
checks; the reference DY2 also passed. Table~\ref{tab:runs} reports
their recorded $\beta$ and ejecta mass. The run identifiers, code
commits, and file hashes are in the
\repo{docs/olcumler/DO_DC_DN_2026-10-06/KAYNAK.md}{[run source record]}.
We use \emph{model ejecta mass} for the simulation quantity; it is not
automatically the same as an observed plume mass.

The four new branches have energy deviations between $-0.79\%$

\repo{docs/defter/KAYIT-076_2026-10-06_DO-DC-DN-uc-kilitli-yargi.md}{[validation record]}.
These checks support run validity; they do not establish
resolution convergence.

\subsection{Observational target}
The mass-adjusted momentum target used for the production scene is
\begin{equation}
\beta_{\mathrm{obs}}(M_{\mathrm{scene}})
=3.5418^{+0.188}_{-0.247}
\quad(M_{\mathrm{scene}}=4.2980\,10^9\ {\rm kg}).
\label{eq:betaobs}
\end{equation}
The asymmetric interval is retained when constructing the likelihood
\repo{docs/adr/ADR-0054-c1-gozlenen-beta-hangi-sayi.md}{[mass adjustment]}.
It is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:results} only as an observational
reference; it is not a posterior interval.

\section{Measured pilot results}
\subsection{Cohesion sweep}
Across the tested $10$--$5000$ Pa span, $\beta$ falls from 3.7480 to
1.8738 while model ejecta mass falls by a factor of 26.1
(Figure~\ref{fig:results}). The predeclared three-point fit
$\beta-1=C\Yzero^p$ gives $p=-0.1791$ and a maximum relative
residual of 0.1198, below the fixed 0.15 fit-quality threshold
\repo{docs/truba/PROTOKOL-DO-DAYANIM-ONSELI.md}{[DO protocol]}.
At the central target in Equation~\ref{eq:betaobs}, this fit
interpolates to $\Yzero\simeq20.1$ Pa (Figure~\ref{fig:atlas}). The value is conditional on the
fixed scene, $\aB$, $f$, material law, and fit form; it is not a
posterior estimate of Dimorphos's strength. The older production
cohesion domain $[10^3,10^7]$ Pa does not contain this conditional
interpolation, motivating the prospectively selected
$[1,10^5]$ Pa domain.

\subsection{Matrix tension}
At $\Yzero=10$ Pa, enabling the tested tension law changes
$\beta$ from 3.7480 to 1.0807. The contrast in excess momentum
$b=\beta-1$ is
\begin{equation}
\frac{|b_{\mathrm{DY2}}-b_{\mathrm{DC}}|}{b_{\mathrm{DY2}}}
=\frac{|2.7480-0.0807|}{2.7480}=0.9706.
\label{eq:tension}
\end{equation}
Recorded model ejecta mass changes from
$1.964\times10^7$ kg to $2.777\times10^2$ kg, a factor of
about $7.1\times10^4$. This is a strong sensitivity to one
constitutive-model choice. Because DC is one toggle at one
parameter location, it does not establish the real body's
tensile strength or rule out all alternative tension laws.
The production model choice remains explicit, and the DC contrast
is reported outside the small-discrepancy denominator
\repo{docs/defter/KAYIT-076_2026-10-06_DO-DC-DN-uc-kilitli-yargi.md}{[locked pilot judgments]}.

\subsection{Impact-site offset}
Moving the impact point 25 m changes $\beta$ from 3.7480 to 3.6650
(2.2\%), but changes model ejecta mass from
$1.964\times10^7$ to $2.321\times10^7$ kg (18\%).
The recorded cone opening changes from $85.2^\circ$ to
$107.7^\circ$, and median ejecta time $t_{50}$ from 0.541 to
0.845 s. A near-stable $\beta$ therefore coexists with a
materially different ejecta field in this controlled pair.
The corresponding impact-location discrepancy on $b$ is
$0.03019$; it exceeds the preregistered \emph{negligible}
boundary of $0.03000$ by $0.00019$. We retain the registered
classification rather than moving that boundary after seeing
the result \repo{docs/defter/KAYIT-076_2026-10-06_DO-DC-DN-uc-kilitli-yargi.md}{[KAYIT-076]}.
Figure~\ref{fig:offset} compares the four paired responses on
a common dimensionless scale and places the five runs in joint
$\beta$--model-ejecta-mass space.

\section{Registered production inference}
\subsection{Design and validation}
The production coordinates are
$\theta=(\aB,\Yzero,f)$, with
$\aB\in[1.00,1.30]$, $\Yzero\in[1,10^5]$ Pa,
and $f\in[0.05,0.50]$. The design has 96 Latin-hypercube
coarse points at 7 m spacing and 12 predetermined nested
reruns at 3.5 m spacing
\repo{docs/adr/ADR-0059-cok-dogruluklu-vekilin-geri-alinmasi.md}{[nested-design decision]}.
Figure~\ref{fig:design} shows coverage; it is a design plot,
not a fitted posterior. The 12 indices are
$\{11,20,33,44,45,57,58,62,74,78,87,92\}$.
Every readable run must be valid, reach 600 s, and meet the
5\% mass-consistency limit. A loss above 20\% invalidates
the pool and triggers its repetition
\repo{docs/truba/PROTOKOL-HAVUZ-URETIM.md}{[pool protocol]}.

The registered surrogate is a Gaussian process validated
in four groups by $\theta$, with predictive variance enlarged
when held-out residuals require it. The residual-correlation
matrix $R$ is measured from those residuals and compared with
$R=I$. The alternative resolution paths are a coarse
surrogate plus measured discrepancy (SIGMA), and an
autoregressive nested coarse/fine surrogate (CD). CD leads
only if the nested design is intact, the fitted coupling
is interior, and held-out fine error is lower than for
SIGMA. Both results are disclosed if they differ materially
\repo{docs/adr/ADR-0058-uretim-posteriorunun-tarifi.md}{[posterior definition]}.
For eligible observation vector $z$ and surrogate mean
$g(\theta)$, the registered heteroskedastic likelihood is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta)\propto
|S(\theta)|^{-1/2}
\exp\!\left[-\tfrac12
(g-z)^{T}S(\theta)^{-1}(g-z)\right].
\label{eq:likelihood}
\end{equation}
The covariance $S(\theta)$ combines predictive, observation,
and admitted model uncertainty with the measured residual
correlation. The determinant term is retained; no production
posterior is evaluated in this report.

\subsection{Observation mapping and model error}
A LICIACube analysis reports an extrapolated plume mass
$(1.6\pm0.3)\times10^7$ kg as a plausible upper limit
for mass within the camera field of view
\cite{lolachi2025}. The model quantity in Table~\ref{tab:runs}
is the reported ejecta statistic at 600 s; the run record
also tracks a separate escaping-mass diagnostic. Neither is definitionally
identical to the observed plume mass, and neither resolves the second
threshold in Figure~\ref{fig:escape}: material fast enough to leave
Dimorphos may still be bound to the binary and return.

The present model $\beta$ is projected along impactor momentum,

computed with verified orbital constants before physical posterior

\repo{docs/truba/PROTOKOL-SI-SON-ISLEME.md}{[post-processing protocol]}.

The predeclared relative discrepancy terms on $b=\beta-1$
are 0.013 (realisation), 0.004 (far-field resolution),
0.053 (near-field resolution), 0.016 (late-time plateau),
0.009 (target shape), and 0.030 (impact location).
The near-field value is documented but its executable
constant is still \texttt{nan}; the code rejects that
configuration. We therefore do not quote a complete
operational denominator. The omission is not a rounding
detail: on the declared values that single term carries
two thirds of the variance of the denominator
(Figure~\ref{fig:paylar}).

\subsection{Checks before a posterior claim}
Simulation-based calibration uses at least 200 replicates
with the actual surrogate and error model; a biased rank
distribution blocks posterior publication \cite{talts2018}.
Local Fisher information is evaluated at no fewer than five
parameter locations. Eigenvalues above 36 count learned
directions, and the smallest count over the registered
correlation sensitivities governs the claim
\repo{docs/truba/PROTOKOL-UP-POSTERIOR.md}{[identifiability protocol]}.
Only two quantitative observation groups are currently locked,
so local rank cannot exceed two. The third candidate,
ejecta-speed distribution, has no locked DART value and
uncertainty.

A shift of more than 0.50 dex in the $\Yzero$ mode under
the declared prior alternatives prevents a single
$\Yzero$ estimate. Independent history matching excludes
points with implausibility $I>3$ \cite{vernon2014}.
The preregistered overlap and volume gates decide whether
a posterior, a broader matching region, or neither is
publishable. These gates are not adjusted after inspection
of the production pool. If less than 90\% of the 68\%
posterior mass lies in the compatible region, both
inferential regions are withheld.

\section{Discussion and conclusions}
The pilot set supports three controlled findings.
\textbf{First}, simulated momentum enhancement and ejecta mass
decrease across the tested cohesion values in one fixed
DART scene. \textbf{Second}, the selected matrix-tension
toggle changes excess momentum by 97.1\% and reduces
the model-reported ejecta statistic by about $7.1\times10^4$
in that scene.
\textbf{Third}, a 25 m impact offset changes $\beta$ by
only 2.2\% while changing ejecta mass, opening angle,
and median ejecta time more. These are model sensitivities,
not a reconstructed interior.

Figure~\ref{fig:levers} puts every lever we have measured on one
scale, and the ordering is the uncomfortable part of this paper. The
single largest measured effect on $b$ is not one of the coordinates we
are trying to infer: it is the matrix-tension law, a constitutive
choice we fixed by decision, at 0.971. It exceeds the entire
10--5000 Pa cohesion span, 0.682, that the inference is supposed to
resolve. Impactor geometry, at 0.134, also sits above the DART
observation's own $1\sigma$ width on $b$. Four further choices fall
below that width and are, on this evidence, not worth arguing about.
A posterior over $(\aB,\Yzero,f)$ is therefore only as meaningful as
the fixed choices standing above it, which is why this paper registers
those choices rather than reporting a posterior.

\subsection*{Relation to published interior studies}
Two recent studies bound what a single $\beta$ can say about an
interior. Raducan and colleagues ran forward simulations across assumed
material sets and interior structures and compared them with the
observations, favouring a low bulk density and a modest near-surface
boulder fraction \cite{raducan2024}. Senel and colleagues then reported
that several distinct interiors reproduce the reference $\beta$: a
homogeneous target with cohesion in the 20--80 Pa range, a
double-layered interior, and a three-layered interior with boulders
clustered at the impact site \cite{senel2025}. That is a degeneracy
statement, and it is the reason the production step here is organised
around how many directions in $(\aB,\Yzero,f)$ the observation actually
constrains rather than around a point estimate.

Our scene-conditional interpolation, 20.1 Pa, falls at the lower edge
of the 20--80 Pa homogeneous range of \cite{senel2025}. We do not treat
that as corroboration. The two calculations differ in code, in target
geometry, in dimensionality, and --- importantly --- in internal
friction, which \cite{senel2025} reports as a strong lever on $\beta$:
their homogeneous range is quoted at friction 0.5, whereas every run in
Table~\ref{tab:runs} uses 0.6. Agreement between two numbers obtained at
different friction may be coincidental. We have not measured our own
sensitivity to that coefficient, and until we do, the comparison is a
consistency note and nothing more.

The principal limits are structural. The three-point
cohesion fit is conditional on the pilot scene. The
near-field discrepancy has not been connected in code,
and that omission is not isolated: the repository's defect
log now records three modules that were written, documented
and unit-tested but never called from the production path,
one of which had been dropped from the posterior definition
before being reinstated
\repo{docs/FAZ4-SIKINTI-RAPORU.md}{[defect A116]}.
A passing test suite and a wired production path are not
the same property, and we have no automated check for the
second.
The plume and model-ejecta definitions differ, as do
the observed and simulated momentum-projection axes.
Crater depth at the present 600 s endpoint is invalid or
physically mismatched to a final settled crater in the
pilot diagnostics
\repo{docs/defter/KAYIT-076_2026-10-06_DO-DC-DN-uc-kilitli-yargi.md}{[crater diagnosis]}.
A single earlier resolution step measured 5.34\%
on $\beta-1$ in a different scene; it is not a convergence
proof \repo{docs/defter/KAYIT-071_2026-09-29_UY2-azaliyor-DY-okunmaz-A112.md}{[resolution record]}.

The Hera protocol defines quantile and hash-sealing
procedures, but the required posterior and validated
crater/shape post-processing are not yet available
\repo{docs/truba/PROTOKOL-HT-HERA-TAHMIN.md}{[Hera protocol]}.
No numerical Hera forecast or production posterior is
reported here.

\section*{Prospective Hera comparison}
Hera will measure Dimorphos's mass, map its crater and surface,
and investigate its interior \cite{esaHera}. The agency reports
arrival in November 2026, a month earlier than originally planned
\cite{esaArrival}. The phases are staggered: shape, mass and rotation
are characterised first, from a distance of tens of kilometres, and
high-resolution crater imaging follows roughly six weeks later. A
sealed prediction is therefore on a different clock for each quantity,
and the shape and mass quantities are the tighter deadline. That is a
scheduling constraint on this project, not a scientific result.
